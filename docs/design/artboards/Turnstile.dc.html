There is no sign of it: the Orchard pool never went negative, so no more value has left than entered. Quiet skimming can’t be ruled out, and the softer signals are muddied by ordinary behaviour.

Yes. In 1,581 days the Orchard pool balance never went negative, and the balance rebuilt from every flow matches the node’s own figure to within 0.00 ZEC.

Has more ever left Orchard than entered it?

No — cumulative in stays above cumulative out the whole way. The gap is the pool balance: 4.31M ZEC at disclosure, 362K today after the Ironwood migration.

Could a fake be hidden in real shields?

No sign of it. An attacker hiding counterfeit deshields behind legitimate shields would make deshields trail shields by a day or two. The correlation peaks at the same day instead, and the profile is roughly symmetric.

Do deshields follow shields?

The peak sits at lag 0 (r = 0.44): shields and deshields rise and fall together with overall activity, which is expected and not a masking fingerprint.

Did anything change after disclosure?

Less than you’d expect from a cash-out. Net of the Ironwood migration the pool kept 86% of its value, and deshielding slowed to 0.8× its prior pace — from 13K to 10K ZEC a day.

A large counterfeit stash would be cashed out fast once the bug was public. The pool did the opposite. A surge would not have proven an attack either: a ~38% price crash makes ordinary holders exit too.

Do the amounts look natural?

Mostly. Leading digits broadly follow Benford’s law. The fit (MAD 0.0160) is just past Nigrini’s 0.015 line for “nonconformity”, driven by a mild excess of amounts starting with 1 — typical of round numbers and exchange batches.

Verdict

Turnstile held
Over 1,581 days the balance never went negative (floor 156 ZEC). Cumulative deshields are 82.6% of cumulative shields, so no unbounded inflation is present and any extraction is capped by the running balance.
Masking signature
Same-day correlation r = 0.44, peaking at lag 0, with a roughly flat profile. That is shared daily activity, not deshields following shields.
After disclosure
Orchard held 4.31M ZEC on 5 Jun 2026 and holds 362K today — 86% retained once the 3.33M ZEC Ironwood migration is excluded. Deshielding slowed to about 0.8× its prior rate (1.32M ZEC out).
Amount distribution
Leading digits broadly track Benford (MAD 0.0160). The mild excess of 1s fits round-number and exchange-batch clustering, not a structuring fingerprint.
Bottom line · 32 / 100
The hard guarantee holds: no net value left Orchard that hadn’t entered it. Every softer signal is real but confounded by legitimate behaviour. This does not prove the bug was never used — skimming within the pool’s own balance is invisible to flow analysis. The honest reading is “no detectable exploitation, and behaviour inconsistent with a large active cash-out” — not “safe”.

Method

How the index and each test are built

Background. On 5 Jun 2026 Shielded Labs disclosed a soundness bug in the Orchard proof circuit: a value-balance constraint written too loosely that, in theory, let an attacker mint counterfeit ZEC inside the pool with no on-chain signature. It was found on 29 May (by Taylor Hornby, using Anthropic’s Opus 4.8) and patched on 1 Jun.

Why the turnstile matters. Minting inside the pool does not change its declared value. A counterfeit only becomes visible when it is deshielded — crossing the turnstile, the consensus rule that a pool can never hold negative value.

The index. A weighted blend of three soft signals — masked-deshield lean (0.5), post-disclosure net drain (0.35) and Benford anomaly (0.15). A turnstile breach overrides everything and pins it to 100.

Data. Orchard’s entire per-block flow history, reconstructed from our Zebra node, starting at Orchard’s launch on 31 May 2022. Deshields ≥ 0.001 ZEC enter the Benford test.
